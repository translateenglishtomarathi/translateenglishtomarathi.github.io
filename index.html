Marathi for Beginners: A Friendly Starting Guide

Translate English to Marathi · Learning Guides · Getting Started

Marathi is the language of Maharashtra — of Mumbai's local trains, Pune's old-city lanes, Kolhapur's wrestlers and the sugarcane fields of the Deccan. Around 83 million people speak it, making it one of the ten most spoken languages on earth, and it carries a literary tradition more than a thousand years old, from the saint-poets of Pandharpur to modern Marathi theatre and cinema. For a learner, the good news is that Marathi is warm, logical and forgiving: people across Maharashtra are delighted when a visitor tries even a few words.

How Marathi sounds

Marathi is written in the Devanagari script, the same family of letters used for Hindi, and its sounds map closely to its spelling — once you can read a word, you can almost always say it correctly. A few sounds need practice: the retroflex ळ (la) that English lacks entirely, and the nasal sounds marked with a dot, as in मंगळवार (mangalvaar — Tuesday). Marathi rhythm is quick and melodic, and everyday speech clips its endings in a friendly, informal way. Do not let that worry you; slow, careful Marathi is understood everywhere.

How Marathi sentences are built

English runs subject–verb–object: "I drink tea." Marathi runs subject–object–verb: मी चहा पितो (mi chahaa pito — literally "I tea drink"). Once your ear accepts the verb at the end, Marathi sentences become predictable. Marathi also uses postpositions — little words that come after the noun instead of before it — so "in the house" is घरात (gharaat), built into the word itself. There are no words for "a" or "the", which removes a whole layer of guesswork.

Respect is built into the grammar

Marathi has three words for "you", and choosing between them is the first social skill to learn. तू (tu) is for children, close friends and God. तुम्ही (tumhi) is the everyday polite form, safe with almost everyone. आपण (aapan) is the most respectful form, used for elders and formal occasions. When in doubt, start with तुम्ही — nobody was ever offended by too much respect. Our accuracy guide explains how this choice affects translation.

The first words to learn

Start with survival words: नमस्कार (namaskar — hello), धन्यवाद (dhanyavaad — thank you), पाणी (paani — water), जेवण (jevan — food, a meal), किती? (kiti? — how much?) and कुठे? (kuthe? — where?). Add the numbers one to ten and you can already bargain in a market, order tea and find a bus stop. Our everyday vocabulary guide gives you sixty of the most useful words in one place, grouped by theme.

Learning the script

Devanagari looks dense at first glance, but it is a system, not a puzzle: a set of consonants, a set of vowels, and vowel signs that attach to consonants in regular ways. Most learners can sound out shop signs and station boards within a few weeks of short daily practice. Our Devanagari script guide walks through the whole system calmly, including the special letter ळ that Marathi is proud of.

A realistic study routine

Fifteen minutes a day beats a monthly marathon. Spend five minutes on the script, five on new words, and five speaking aloud — even to yourself. Use the translator on this site to check sentences about your own life: your family, your job, your town. Personal sentences stick in memory far better than textbook ones. And speak from the first week: a single नमस्कार at a Pune tea stall will teach you more about Maharashtrian hospitality than a chapter of grammar. Dhanyavaad — and welcome to the language.

The Devanagari Script for Marathi: Letters, Signs and the Special ळ

Translate English to Marathi · Learning Guides · The Script

Marathi is written in Devanagari, the elegant script with the horizontal line running along the top of every word. It is one of the most scientific writing systems ever designed: letters are arranged by where in the mouth each sound is made, and spelling follows pronunciation so faithfully that Marathi children learn to read by simply sounding words out. If you have seen Hindi writing, you already know the shapes — Marathi uses the same script with a few proud differences of its own.

The vowels (स्वर)

The independent vowels are: अ a, आ aa, इ i, ई ee, उ u, ऊ oo, ऋ ru, ए e, ऐ ai, ओ o, औ au, plus the nasal sign अं (am) and the breathy अः (ah). Every consonant carries the vowel "a" inside it by default: क is not just "k" but "ka". This single idea unlocks the whole script.

The consonants (व्यंजन)

The consonants are laid out in a grid by mouth position. From the throat: क ka, ख kha, ग ga, घ gha, ङ nga. From the palate: च cha, छ chha, ज ja, झ jha, ञ nya. With the tongue curled back: ट ta, ठ tha, ड da, ढ dha, ण na. With the tongue at the teeth: त ta, थ tha, द da, ध dha, न na. From the lips: प pa, फ pha, ब ba, भ bha, म ma. Then the flowing sounds: य ya, र ra, ल la, व va, the sibilants श sha, ष sha, स sa, and ह ha.

The special letter ळ

Marathi's signature letter is ळ (la) — a retroflex "l" made by curling the tongue back against the palate. It appears in some of the language's most everyday words: केळं (kelam — banana), मंगळवार (mangalvaar — Tuesday), पळ (pal — run). Hindi dropped this sound, so its presence in a word is a quick way to tell Marathi writing from Hindi at a glance. Learners often substitute a plain ल at first; listeners understand, and the real sound comes with practice.

Vowel signs (मात्रा) and joined letters

When a vowel follows a consonant inside a word, it shrinks into a sign attached to that consonant: का kaa, कि ki, की kee, कु ku, के ke, को ko. A dot above, the anusvara, adds a nasal sound, as in मंगळवार. When two consonants meet with no vowel between them, they join into a combined shape: क्ष (ksha) in शिक्षण (shikshan — education) and ज्ञ (dnya) in ज्ञान (dnyaan — knowledge). There are only a handful of common joined letters, and you will meet them again and again.

One Marathi habit: the open "a"

Marathi also uses a special sign for the open "a" sound in English loanwords: कॅ as in कॅफे (cafe) and बॅट (bat). You will see it on signboards everywhere — a small modern addition to a very old script.

How to learn it

Learn the vowels first, then one row of consonants a day, then the vowel signs. Read real things — station boards, bus destinations, shop names — and check doubtful words in the translator. Pair this guide with our beginners guide and you have the complete foundation: script, sounds and sentence order working together.

How to Translate English to Marathi Accurately

Translate English to Marathi · Learning Guides · Better Results

Machine translation between English and Marathi has improved enormously, but the two languages make very different choices, so small habits on your side make a big difference to the result. These nine tips come from the errors we see most often.

1. Write complete sentences

Fragments confuse translators because Marathi verb endings carry information English leaves to context. "Coming tomorrow" could be a question, a plan or a command. Write "I am coming tomorrow" and the Marathi comes out clean: मी उद्या येतो आहे (mi udya yeto aahe).

2. Keep sentences short

Marathi puts the verb last, so a forty-word English sentence becomes a long walk to the verb. Split long sentences into two or three short ones before translating; the output will be dramatically more natural, and any mistake will be easier to spot.

3. Decide who "you" is

English "you" hides a choice Marathi cannot avoid. तू (tu) is intimate — children, close friends, family. तुम्ही (tumhi) is the respectful everyday form. आपण (aapan) is the most formal. If your English text is for an elder, a teacher or an official, add "sir", "madam" or "please" to the sentence; translators usually respond by choosing the respectful Marathi forms and verb endings.

4. Remember Marathi has three genders

Every Marathi noun is masculine, feminine or neuter, and adjectives and verbs agree with it. English gives the translator no hint, so "the house is big" may come back with the wrong ending for घर (ghar — house, neuter). If a result looks off, the gender of one noun is the first thing to check. Our sentence-building guide shows how agreement works.

5. Use punctuation properly

Commas and full stops tell the translator where ideas end. A missing full stop can glue two thoughts into one strange Marathi sentence. Question marks matter too — Marathi question words like काय (kaay — what) are placed by meaning, and your "?" helps the translator find the question.

6. Avoid slang and idioms

"It's raining cats and dogs" translated word by word produces very confused Marathi. Rewrite idioms plainly first — "it is raining heavily" — then translate. The meaning survives; the confusion does not.

7. Be careful with names and places

Translators sometimes translate names that happen to be ordinary words. If a person's or company's name must stay in English letters, translate the sentence in two parts and paste the name back in afterwards. Place names in Maharashtra are usually written in Devanagari in the output, which is correct for local use.

8. Watch English words with several meanings

"Book" the noun (पुस्तक, pustak) and "book" the verb (reserve) are different Marathi words. If the result looks odd, add a clarifying word in English — "book a ticket", "read a book" — and translate again.

9. Back-translate to check

Finally, paste the Marathi result back into the translator in the Marathi-to-English direction. If the English that returns matches what you meant, your translation is safe to send. Try it now on the translator — and keep our travel phrases handy if your translation is for a Maharashtra trip.

Essential Marathi Phrases for Travelling in Maharashtra

Translate English to Marathi · Learning Guides · Travel

Maharashtra rewards travellers who try even a little Marathi. English and Hindi are widely understood in Mumbai and Pune, but a greeting in Marathi at a railway station, a village dhaba or a temple queue changes the whole conversation — people slow down, smile, and go out of their way to help. Here are the phrases that matter most, grouped by the moments you will actually use them.

Greetings and courtesy

Start every interaction with नमस्कार (namaskar — hello), hands joined if you like. Ask तुम्ही कसे आहात? (tumhi kase aahaat? — how are you?) and answer मी ठीक आहे (mi theek aahe — I am fine). Thank everyone with धन्यवाद (dhanyavaad); it is short, warm and always noticed. When leaving, पुन्हा भेटूया (punha bhetuya — let us meet again) is the friendliest goodbye in the language.

Getting around

For trains, buses and rickshaws, one question settles most things: हे कुठे आहे? (he kuthe aahe? — where is this?). Ask the fare before you ride: भाडे किती आहे? (bhaade kiti aahe?). In the vehicle, इथे थांबा (ithe thaamba — stop here), डावीकडे (daaveekade — to the left) and उजवीकडे (ujveekade — to the right) handle directions, and हळू चालवा (halu chaalavaa — please drive slowly) is worth its weight in gold on ghat roads.

Food and drink

Maharashtra eats well and often. Start the day with पोहे (pohe — flattened rice) and a cutting चहा (chahaa — tea). At lunch, जेवण मिळेल का? (jevan milel kaa? — can I get a meal?) opens any small hotel's kitchen. Declare मी शाकाहारी आहे (mi shaakaahaari aahe — I am vegetarian) if needed, ask for कमी तिखट (kami tikhat — less spicy) if Kolhapuri heat is too much, and praise the cook with खूप छान आहे (khoop chhaan aahe — it is very good).

Shopping and bargaining

हे कितीला आहे? (he kiteela aahe? — how much is this?) opens every negotiation. If the price feels high, smile and say कमी करा (kami karaa — reduce it a little). Bargaining in Maharashtra's markets is a conversation, not a contest — our shopping guide walks through the whole exchange.

Emergencies and help

Memorise three lines just in case: मदत करा! (madat karaa! — help!), मला बरे वाटत नाही (mala bare vaatata naahi — I am not well) and रुग्णालय कुठे आहे? (rugnaalay kuthe aahe? — where is the hospital?). India's emergency number is 112 everywhere in Maharashtra. People here are quick to help a traveller in trouble — our emergency guide has the full set.

Marathi Numbers 1 to 100: Counting Made Simple

Translate English to Marathi · Learning Guides · Numbers & Counting

Numbers are among the first things you need in any language. In Maharashtra you will use them for rickshaw fares, market prices, platform numbers, phone numbers and train times — often several times a day. Marathi counting has one honest warning attached: the numbers between eleven and ninety-nine are famously irregular, each with its own name. Locals will tell you this with pride. Learn the pattern in stages and you will be fine.

The numbers 1 to 10

Everything starts here: एक (ek — one), दोन (don — two), तीन (teen — three), चार (chaar — four), पाच (paach — five), सहा (sahaa — six), सात (saat — seven), आठ (aath — eight), नऊ (nau — nine) and दहा (dahaa — ten). Say them aloud a few times; vendors will recognise your effort instantly. In Devanagari digits these are written १ २ ३ ४ ५ ६ ७ ८ ९ १०, and you will see both digit systems side by side on price boards and number plates.

The teens and tens

The teens run: अकरा (akraa — eleven), बारा (baaraa — twelve), तेरा (teraa — thirteen), चौदा (chaudaa — fourteen), पंधरा (pandhraa — fifteen), सोळा (solaa — sixteen), सतरा (satraa — seventeen), अठरा (athraa — eighteen) and एकोणीस (ekonees — nineteen). The tens: वीस (vees — twenty), तीस (tees — thirty), चाळीस (chaalees — forty), पन्नास (pannaas — fifty), साठ (saath — sixty), सत्तर (sattar — seventy), ऐंशी (ainshi — eighty) and नव्वद (navvad — ninety). Unlike English, Marathi does not build "twenty-five" from twenty and five — 25 is its own word, पंचवीस (panchvees). Learn the tens first, then pick up the in-between numbers as you meet them in real prices.

Hundreds, thousands and lakhs

For bigger numbers: शंभर (shambhar — one hundred), हजार (hazaar — one thousand) and लाख (laakh — one lakh, that is, one hundred thousand). India counts in lakhs and crores rather than millions, so a price quoted as पन्नास लाख (pannaas laakh) means fifty lakh rupees — five million. Knowing this one word prevents expensive misunderstandings when people discuss salaries, flats and wedding budgets.

Numbers in daily life

A few ready-made lines cover most situations. Asking a price: हे कितीला आहे? (he kiteela aahe? — how much is this?). Zero is शून्य (shoonya), which you will need for phone numbers, given digit by digit. Days, Months and Telling the Time in Marathi

Translate English to Marathi · Learning Guides · Time & Dates

"When?" is one of the most practical questions in any language. Whether you are catching a train from Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus, fixing a meeting in Pune or planning around the monsoon, you need days, dates and clock times. Marathi keeps the everyday side of this simple: a small set of words covers almost every plan you will make.

The seven days

The week runs: रविवार (ravivaar — Sunday), सोमवार (somvaar — Monday), मंगळवार (mangalvaar — Tuesday), बुधवार (budhvaar — Wednesday), गुरुवार (guruvaar — Thursday), शुक्रवार (shukravaar — Friday) and शनिवार (shanivaar — Saturday). Each is named after a heavenly body or deity — the sun, the moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus and Saturn — just as the English words Sunday and Monday are. Notice the retroflex ळ hiding inside Tuesday's name.

Today, tomorrow and yesterday

Three words handle most planning: आज (aaj — today), उद्या (udya — tomorrow) and काल (kaal — yesterday). Marathi uses उद्या for tomorrow and also for the day after in some contexts, and परवा (parvaa) covers both "the day after tomorrow" and "the day before yesterday" — context tells you which. "See you tomorrow" is उद्या भेटूया (udya bhetuya), a phrase worth using daily.

Parts of the day

The day divides into सकाळ (sakaal — morning), दुपार (dupaar — afternoon), संध्याकाळ (sandhyakaal — evening) and रात्र (raatra — night). Greetings follow the same pattern — शुभ सकाळ (shubh sakaal) for good morning and शुभ रात्री (shubh raatri) for good night.

Months of the year

For everyday dates, Maharashtra uses the familiar English month names. But Marathi also keeps its own traditional calendar, whose months appear on festival posters, panchang almanacs and wedding invitations: चैत्र (chaitra, roughly mid-March to mid-April) opens the year with Gudi Padwa, followed by वैशाख (vaishaakh), ज्येष्ठ (jyeshtha), आषाढ (aashaadh — when the Pandharpur pilgrimage walks), श्रावण (shraavan — the monsoon month of festivals), भाद्रपद (bhaadrapad — Ganesh Chaturthi), आश्विन (aashvin), कार्तिक (kaartik), मार्गशीर्ष (maargasheersha), पौष (paush), माघ (maagh) and फाल्गुन (phaalgun — Holi).

Asking and telling the time

Ask किती वाजले? (kiti vaajle? — what time is it?, literally "how many have struck?"). Answers use the verb वाजणे (vaajne — to strike): दहा वाजले (dahaa vaajle — it is ten o'clock). Half past is साडे (saade): साडे तीन (saade teen) is half past three. An hour is एक तास (ek taas), a minute is एक मिनिट (ek minute), and the word for time itself is वेळ (vel).

Useful time expressions

A few more expressions round out your planning vocabulary: आठवडा (aathavdaa — week), महिना (mahinaa — month), वर्ष (varsha — year), रोज (roj — every day, daily) and नेहमी (nehmi — always). "Next week" is पुढच्या आठवड्यात (pudhchyaa aathavdyaat), and if someone says थोडा वेळ थांबा (thoda vel thaamba — wait a little while), settle in: Maharashtrian "a little while" is a flexible measure.

Family Words in Marathi: Relatives and Relationships

Translate English to Marathi · Learning Guides · Family

Family sits at the centre of life in Maharashtra, and Marathi has a precise word for almost every relationship — including which side of the family a relative belongs to, a distinction English simply ignores. Learning these words does more than build vocabulary: it explains why half the people in a Pune street seem to be someone's "kaka" (uncle) or "mavshi" (aunt), related or not. In Maharashtra, respectful family words are used for elders everywhere — the vegetable seller, the bus conductor, the neighbour — and using them earns instant warmth.

Parents and grandparents

Father is वडील (vadeel) — also called बाबा (baabaa) at home — and mother is आई (aai), one of the most beloved words in the language. Grandfather is आजोबा (aajoba) and grandmother is आजी (aaji); you will hear children in every building call out to them. The general word for family itself is कुटुंब (kutumb), and relatives are नातेवाईक (naatevaaik).

Brothers and sisters

An elder brother is भाऊ (bhaau) — also the everyday respectful word for any man of your generation — and an elder sister is ताई (taai), used just as widely for women. A younger brother or sister is simply addressed by name, but the words are धाकटा भाऊ (dhaakataa bhaau — younger brother) and धाकटी बहीण (dhaakati baheen — younger sister), while बहीण (baheen) is sister in general. A brother's wife is वहिनी (vahini) — a relationship Maharashtrians treat with particular affection and respect.

Uncles and aunts

Here Marathi is more detailed than English. Your father's younger brother is काका (kaakaa) and his wife is काकू (kaaku); your father's elder brother is मोठे काका or बडे बाबा in many families. Your mother's brother is मामा (maamaa) and his wife मामी (maami). Your father's sister is आत्या (aatyaa), and your mother's sister is मावशी (maavshi) — in everyday speech, almost any woman of your mother's generation may be called mavshi, and any older man kaka. Do not worry about memorising every branch at once; people are forgiving teachers.

Children and the next generation

A son is मुलगा (mulga) and a daughter is मुलगी (mulgi); children together are मुले (mule). A husband is नवरा (navra) and a wife is बायको (baayko). In-laws have their own set: mother-in-law सासू (saasu), father-in-law सासरे (saasre), daughter-in-law सून (soon) and son-in-law जावई (jaavai). Grandchildren are नातू (naatu — grandson) and नात (naat — granddaughter).

Talking about your family

Two sentence frames cover most introductions. "This is my mother" is ही माझी आई आहे (hi maajhi aai aahe). "I have two sisters" is मला दोन बहिणी आहेत (mala don baheeni aahet). Notice माझा / माझी / माझे (maajhaa / maajhi / maajhe — my) changing with the gender of the thing owned; both return in our self-introduction guide.

Family questions are friendly conversation in Maharashtra, not prying — asking about someone's कुटुंब shows you care. Practise your answers in the translator, and you will be ready for the warmest small talk in India.

Marathi Greetings and Goodbyes for Every Occasion

Translate English to Marathi · Learning Guides · Speaking

Every conversation in Maharashtra begins and ends with warmth, and the words are easy to learn. Master this small set of greetings and goodbyes and you will never be stuck at the start or the end of a chat again — in a home, a shop, an office or on the phone.

The all-purpose namaskar

नमस्कार (namaskar) works at any hour, for any age, in any setting — hello, good day and respectful greeting in one word. Join your palms lightly as you say it and you have the complete Marathi greeting. When someone greets you first, simply return the same word with a smile. Namaskar is also what you say when entering a temple or an elder's home, which makes it the single most useful word in this guide. With very respected elders, some people say नमस्कार, पाया पडतो (namaskar, paayaa padto — I touch your feet), a gesture of deep respect you may receive but are never obliged to return as a guest.

Greetings by time of day

Marathi also has time-specific greetings: शुभ सकाळ (shubh sakaal — good morning), शुभ संध्या (shubh sandhya — good evening) and शुभ रात्री (shubh raatri — good night). In casual daily life, plain namaskar is more common than all three — you will hear it at 9 in the morning and 9 at night alike.

Asking how someone is

The standard question is तुम्ही कसे आहात? (tumhi kase aahaat? — how are you?), with the shorter कसं काय? (kasam kaay? — how's it going?) between people who know each other well. The usual answer is मी ठीक आहे (mi theek aahe — I am fine), or the cheerful मस्त! (mast! — great!). To ask after someone's family, add घरी सगळे कसे आहेत? (ghari sagle kase aahet? — how is everyone at home?) — a question that is always appreciated.

On the phone

Phone calls open with हॅलो (hello) or namaskar. To ask who is speaking: कोण बोलतंय? (kon boltay? — who is speaking?). To say "I will call back", use मी पुन्हा फोन करतो (mi punha phone karto). Calls to elders and offices often end with a respectful namaskar rather than a casual bye.

Welcome and congratulations

Welcome a guest with स्वागत आहे (swaagat aahe — welcome). Congratulate with अभिनंदन (abhinandan — congratulations), wish a birthday with वाढदिवसाच्या शुभेच्छा (vaadhdivsaachya shubhechha), and thank someone warmly with खूप धन्यवाद (khoop dhanyavaad — thank you very much). Festival wishes get their own guide — see our festivals article.

Saying goodbye — Marathi style

The classic parting is येतो / येते (yeto / yete — literally "I shall come [again]"), said by men and women respectively as they take leave; the reply is या (yaa — do come). Between friends, पुन्हा भेटूया (punha bhetuya — let us meet again) is the natural goodbye. The idea underneath is the same as the greeting: nobody really leaves; they only go and return.

Try one greeting today — at a shop, with a neighbour, anywhere — and build the habit with the phrasebook. For meeting someone properly for the first time, continue to our self-introduction guide.

Shopping and Bargaining in Marathi: Market Phrases That Work

Translate English to Marathi · Learning Guides · Shopping

Maharashtra's markets — the flower garlands of Dadar, the Paithani silk shops of Paithan, the Alphonso mango crates of Ratnagiri season — run on friendly talk. Prices in small shops and weekly bazaars are often a conversation, not a label. These phrases will help you shop with confidence and bargain with a smile, which is exactly how it is done locally.

Entering and looking around

Start with नमस्कार (namaskar). If you are just browsing, say फक्त बघतो आहे (phakta baghto aahe — I am just looking). To ask for something specific: दाखवा (daakhvaa — please show me) followed by the item, or यात दुसरा रंग आहे का? (yaat dusra rang aahe kaa? — do you have this in another colour?). The word for shop is दुकान (dukaan) and for market, बाजार (baazaar).

Asking the price

The one question you will use everywhere: हे कितीला आहे? (he kiteela aahe? — how much is this?). For things sold by weight, ask किलो कितीला? (kilo kiteela? — how much per kilo?). Answers come fast, so learn the numbers first. If you did not catch the reply, पुन्हा सांगा (punha saanga — please say it again) buys you a slower repeat. The word for price itself is किंमत (kimmat), expensive is महाग (mahaag) and cheap is स्वस्त (swasta).

Sizes, colours and trying things

For clothes and footwear: मोठा साईज आहे का? (motha size aahe kaa? — do you have a bigger size?) and लहान साईज (lahaan size — smaller size). Common colours: लाल (laal — red), निळा (nilaa — blue), हिरवा (hirvaa — green), काळा (kaalaa — black), पांढरा (paandhraa — white) and पिवळा (pivlaa — yellow). Big and small in general are मोठा (motha) and लहान (lahaan) — remember their endings change with the gender of the thing described.

Bargaining, politely

In markets (less so in fixed-price shops), gentle bargaining — घासाघीस (ghaasaaghees) — is expected and even enjoyed. Open with किंमत जरा जास्त आहे (kimmat jaraa jaasta aahe — the price is a little high), then ask कमी करा (kami karaa — reduce it). Accept with चालेल, हे घ्या (chaalel, he ghyaa — fine, I will take this), or decline kindly with नको, धन्यवाद (nako, dhanyavaad — no need, thank you). Keep it light: a joke in broken Marathi is worth more than a hard face.

Paying and finishing

Ask for the bill with बिल द्या (bill dya). Cash is पैसे (paise); the change you receive back is सुटे पैसे (sute paise). Most shops now accept UPI payments — यूपीआय चालेल का? (UPI chaalel kaa? — will UPI work?) is a perfectly normal question in 2026.

Keep this guide open on market day alongside the phrasebook, and run any tricky sentence through the translator before you set out. Bargaining in Marathi, even imperfect Marathi, changes the whole shopping experience.

Emergency and Health Phrases in Marathi

Translate English to Marathi · Learning Guides · Emergency

Nobody plans an emergency, but a few memorised lines can make a frightening moment manageable. Maharashtra has good hospitals in every district town and helpful people everywhere; these phrases make sure you can ask for the right help quickly, for yourself or for someone else. Save this page offline before you travel.

Calling for help

The phrase that cuts through noise is मदत करा! (madat karaa! — help!). Add धोका आहे! (dhokaa aahe! — there is danger!) if there has been a crash or a fall, and थांबा! (thaamba! — stop!) to halt someone urgently. India's single emergency number is 112, and it works across Maharashtra for police, fire and ambulance — save it in your phone alongside these words.

Getting medical help

The essentials: रुग्णालय कुठे आहे? (rugnaalay kuthe aahe? — where is the hospital?), डॉक्टरला बोलवा (doctorla bolavaa — please call a doctor), and अँब्युलन्स बोलवा (ambulance bolavaa — call an ambulance). The word for a doctor is डॉक्टर (doctor) and for a hospital, रुग्णालय (rugnaalay) or the everyday दवाखाना (davaakhaana — clinic, dispensary).

Describing what is wrong

Start with मला बरे वाटत नाही (mala bare vaatata naahi — I am not well). The word दुखणे (dukhne — pain, aching) combines with body parts: डोके दुखते (doke dukhte — headache, literally "the head aches"), पोट दुखते (pot dukhte — stomach ache), दात दुखतो (daat dukhto — toothache). Other common words: ताप (taap — fever), खोकला (khoklaa — cough), रक्त (rakta — blood) and अ‍ॅलर्जी (allergy). "It hurts here" is इथे दुखते (ithe dukhte) — point as you say it.

At the pharmacy

Medicine is औषध (aushadh), and a pharmacy is usually signed "Medical" or औषध दुकान (aushadh dukaan). Useful lines: हे औषध आहे का? (he aushadh aahe kaa? — do you have this medicine?) and दिवसातून किती वेळा घ्यायचे? (divsaatun kiti velaa ghyaayche? — how many times a day should I take it?).

Police and personal safety

For theft or trouble: पोलिसांना बोलवा (polisaanna bolavaa — call the police). "I have lost my bag" is माझी बॅग हरवली (maajhi bag haravali), and "I don't understand" — मला समजले नाही (mala samajle naahi) — is the honest phrase that slows any difficult conversation down to a manageable speed.

At the hospital desk

Once you reach care, a few administrative phrases smooth the way: माझे नाव ___ आहे (maajhe naav ___ aahe — my name is ___), मला ___ ची अ‍ॅलर्जी आहे (mala ___ chi allergy aahe — I am allergic to ___), and मी हे औषध घेतो / घेते (mi he aushadh gheto / ghete — I take this medicine). Marathi Question Words and Pronouns: Ask Anything

Translate English to Marathi · Learning Guides · Speaking

Questions are where a language starts working for you. With a handful of question words and pronouns you can ask for prices, directions, names and help anywhere in Maharashtra — and understand most of the answers. This guide gives you the complete set, with examples you can use today.

The question words

  • काय? (kaay?) — What? हे काय आहे? (he kaay aahe? — what is this?)
  • कोण? (kon?) — Who? ते कोण आहेत? (te kon aahet? — who is that person?)
  • कुठे? (kuthe?) — Where? स्टेशन कुठे आहे? (station kuthe aahe? — where is the station?)
  • कधी? (kadhi?) — When? गाडी कधी येईल? (gaadi kadhi yeil? — when will the train come?)
  • का? (kaa?) — Why? का उशीर झाला? (kaa usheer jhaala? — why was there a delay?)
  • कसे? (kase?) — How? तिथे कसे जायचे? (tithe kase jaayche? — how do I go there?)
  • कोणते? (konte?) — Which? कोणते चांगले आहे? (konte chaangle aahe? — which is good?)
  • किती? (kiti?) — How much / how many? हे कितीला आहे? (he kiteela aahe? — how much is this?)

The kaa trick: yes-no questions

Marathi often makes a yes–no question by adding the word का (kaa) at the end of a statement. तुम्ही पुण्याला जाता (tumhi punyaalaa jaataa — you are going to Pune) becomes तुम्ही पुण्याला जाता का? (tumhi punyaalaa jaataa kaa? — are you going to Pune?). You already know it from चहा घेणार का? (chahaa ghenaar kaa? — will you have tea?) — quite possibly the most frequently asked question in Maharashtra.

The pronouns

  • मी (mi) — I
  • तू (tu) — you (close: friends, children, family)
  • तुम्ही (tumhi) — you (polite, and also plural)
  • आपण (aapan) — you (most respectful) / we including the listener
  • तो (to) — he · ती (ti) — she · ते (te) — it, that (neuter)
  • हे (he) — this (neuter) · हा (haa) — this (masculine) · ही (hi) — this (feminine)
  • आम्ही (aamhi) — we (not including the listener)
  • ते / त्या / ती (te / tyaa / ti) — they, by gender

The two "we" words confuse learners at first, but the idea is friendly: आपण (aapan) invites the listener into the group — "let's eat" thinking — while आम्ही (aamhi) talks about your own group. And the three forms of "this" remind you of Marathi's three genders: masculine हा, feminine ही, neuter हे. Our sentence guide shows how these ripple through a whole sentence.

Putting it together

Notice how direct Marathi questions can be: तुमचे नाव काय आहे? (tumche naav kaay aahe? — what is your name?) is just "your name — what is?". Build your own questions by combining this guide with the numbers and vocabulary lists, and test them in the translator before you use them.

Festivals in Maharashtra are street processions, drum troupes, sweets pressed into your hands by neighbours, and whole cities that stop to celebrate together. Ganesh Chaturthi — the state's beloved festival

For ten days in भाद्रपद (bhaadrapad), Maharashtra belongs to Lord Ganesh. Families bring home clay idols, neighbourhoods raise enormous public pandals, and the air rings with one chant: "गणपती बाप्पा मोरया!" (ganpati bappa morya! — victory to Lord Ganesh!). His favourite sweet, the steamed rice मोदक (modak), appears in every home — try the coconut-jaggery उकडीचे मोदक (ukdiche modak) if you are offered one. Greet everyone with गणेश चतुर्थीच्या शुभेच्छा (ganesh chaturthichyaa shubhechha — happy Ganesh Chaturthi), and on the farewell day join the crowds calling "पुढच्या वर्षी लवकर या!" (pudhchyaa varshi lavkar yaa! — come back early next year!).

Gudi Padwa — the Marathi New Year

गुढी पाडवा (gudi paadvaa), on the first day of चैत्र (chaitra), is the traditional Marathi New Year. Homes raise a गुढी (gudi) — a bright silk cloth and an upturned copper pot on a bamboo stick — from windows and terraces as a flag of victory and prosperity. Families eat neem leaves with jaggery, a deliberate mix of bitter and sweet to begin the year. Wish everyone गुढी पाडव्याच्या शुभेच्छा (gudi paadvyaachyaa shubhechha) or the general New Year greeting नववर्षाच्या शुभेच्छा (navavarshaachyaa shubhechha).

Makar Sankranti — tilgul and sweet words

In mid-January, Maharashtra exchanges small sesame-and-jaggery sweets called तीळगूळ (tilgul) with a fixed, lovely formula: "तीळगूळ घ्या, गोड गोड बोला" (tilgul ghyaa, god god bolaa — take this tilgul, and speak sweetly). It is an apology, a peace offering and a blessing in one sentence — if someone hands you tilgul, accept it and repeat the words back.

Dahi Handi and Ashadhi Ekadashi

For Krishna's birthday, teams of young men and women — गोविंदा (govinda) — build human pyramids to break a clay pot of curd hung high above the street, to cries of "गोविंदा आला रे!" (govinda aalaa re! — Govinda has come!). In a quieter key, आषाढी एकादशी (aashaadhi ekaadashi) sees lakhs of वारकरी (vaarkari) pilgrims walk for weeks to Pandharpur, singing the abhangs of Sant Tukaram — the devotional heartbeat of Maharashtra.

Diwali — lights, faral and Lakshmi puja

Diwali in Maharashtra means dawn oil baths, paper lanterns called आकाशकंदील (aakaashkandeel), rows of clay lamps, and फराळ (pharaal) — the festival snack spread of chakli, chivda, ladoo and karanji that every home prepares and shares. Greet with दिवाळीच्या शुभेच्छा (diwaalichyaa shubhechha — happy Diwali), and accept whatever faral you are offered; refusing is nearly impossible anyway.

The one phrase for every festival

If you remember nothing else, the pattern ___च्या शुभेच्छा (___chyaa shubhechha — happy ___) plus the festival's name works all year round. How to Introduce Yourself in Marathi

Translate English to Marathi · Learning Guides · Speaking

A confident self-introduction is the highest-value minute of Marathi you can learn. It answers the questions every new acquaintance in Maharashtra will ask — your name, your place, your work — and it turns strangers into hosts. Build yours line by line below, then say the whole thing aloud a few times.

Start with your name

Open with नमस्कार (namaskar), then माझे नाव ___ आहे (maajhe naav ___ aahe — my name is ___). If someone asks तुमचे नाव काय आहे? (tumche naav kaay aahe? — what is your name?), the same sentence is your answer. Names are not translated; simply place yours in the gap.

Say where you are from

Use मी ___ चा / ची आहे (mi ___ chaa / chi aahe — I am from ___): मी लंडनचा आहे (mi londonchaa aahe — I am from London, said by a man; a woman says लंडनची, londonchi). For where you live now: मी आता पुण्यात राहतो (mi aataa punyaat raahto — I now live in Pune; women say राहते, raahte). Notice how the little endings change with the speaker — Marathi verbs and adjectives agree with gender, and listeners hear it as natural correctness, not pedantry.

Talk about your work

The pattern is मी ___ आहे (mi ___ aahe — I am a ___): मी शिक्षक आहे (mi shikshak aahe — I am a teacher), मी डॉक्टर आहे (mi doctor aahe — I am a doctor). A student says मी विद्यार्थी आहे (mi vidyaarthi aahe) — विद्यार्थिनी (vidyaarthini) for a woman. Not sure of your job's Marathi name? Check it in the translator — professions translate cleanly, and many are simply the English word in Devanagari dress.

Mention your family

Family talk is welcome talk here. "I have one elder sister" is मला एक ताई आहे (mala ek taai aahe). Married speakers can add माझे लग्न झाले आहे (maajhe lagna jhaale aahe — I am married). The full family vocabulary lives in our family guide.

Add your languages — and a humble line

Two honest lines that always win smiles: मी मराठी शिकतो आहे (mi marathi shikto aahe — I am learning Marathi) and मला थोडी मराठी येते (mala thodi marathi yete — I know a little Marathi). People will slow down, help you, and often teach you a word on the spot.

A complete example

Put together, a full introduction sounds like this: नमस्कार, माझे नाव अ‍ॅलेक्स आहे. मी लंडनचा आहे आणि आता पुण्यात राहतो. मी शिक्षक आहे. मला एक ताई आहे. मी मराठी शिकतो आहे — मला थोडी मराठी येते. तुम्हाला भेटून आनंद झाला, धन्यवाद! (namaskar, maajhe naav Alex aahe. mi londonchaa aahe aani aataa punyaat raahto. mi shikshak aahe. mala ek taai aahe. mi marathi shikto aahe — mala thodi marathi yete. tumhaala bhetoon aanand jhaala, dhanyavaad!) — Hello, my name is Alex. I am from London and now live in Pune. I am a teacher. I have one elder sister. I am learning Marathi — I know a little Marathi. Nice to meet you, thank you! Sixty seconds, and you have made a friend.

Close politely

Finish with तुम्हाला भेटून आनंद झाला (tumhaala bhetoon aanand jhaala — nice to meet you) and धन्यवाद (dhanyavaad — thank you). That is a complete, respectful introduction in under a minute. Practise yours with the phrases in the phrasebook, and you are ready for any first meeting in Maharashtra.

Marathi for Food Lovers: Ordering Food in Maharashtra

Translate English to Marathi · Learning Guides · Food & Drink

Food is Maharashtra's love language, and even a little Marathi transforms eating here — waiters light up, portions grow, and the best off-menu items appear. This guide covers the words for the dishes you will meet and the phrases that get them made your way.

Breakfast: pohe, upma and cutting chai

The Maharashtrian morning runs on पोहे (pohe — flattened rice tossed with onion, turmeric and peanuts), उपमा (upma — savoury semolina) and इडली (idli) in the south-influenced canteens, all washed down with चहा (chahaa — tea), ordered as a कटिंग (cutting — a half glass, strong and sweet) in Mumbai. Order with एक प्लेट पोहे (ek plate pohe — one plate of pohe).

The street classics

Mumbai's gift to the world is the वडापाव (vada pav) — a spiced potato fritter in a bun with garlic chutney — best ordered एक वडापाव, तिखट (ek vada pav, tikhat — one vada pav, spicy). Pune answers with मिसळ पाव (misal pav — fiery sprouted-bean curry with bread and crunchy farsan on top), while Kolhapur's misal is a whole different level of heat. Add पावभाजी (pav bhaji — mashed spiced vegetables with buttered rolls) and भजी (bhaji — onion fritters, mandatory in the monsoon) and you have the street canon.

The village thali: pithla bhakri

The soul food of rural Maharashtra is पिठलं भाकरी (pithlam bhaakri) — a gram-flour curry with a hand-patted millet or sorghum flatbread, served with raw onion, green chilli and sometimes झुणका (zunka), its drier cousin. A full meal is simply जेवण (jevan): bhakri or chapati, भात (bhaat — rice), आमटी (aamti — sweet-sour dal), a भाजी (bhaaji — vegetable) and pickle. Say जेवण मिळेल का? (jevan milel kaa? — can I get a meal?) at any small khanaval around noon and you will be fed like family.

Sweets: puran poli and modak

For festivals and celebrations: पुरणपोळी (puran poli — a soft flatbread stuffed with sweet dal and jaggery, eaten with ghee or warm milk), मोदक (modak — Ganesh's rice-flour dumplings), श्रीखंड (shrikhand — saffron hung-curd dessert, glorious with mango as आम्रखंड, aamrakhand) and बासुंदी (baasundi — thickened sweet milk). In mango season, आंबा (aamba) rules everything, and आमरस (aamras — mango pulp) is eaten by the bowl.

Making it your way

The control phrases: कमी तिखट (kami tikhat — less spicy), कमी गोड (kami god — less sweet), शाकाहारी (shaakaahaari — vegetarian) and मांसाहारी (maansaahaari — non-vegetarian). Praise with खूप छान आहे! (khoop chhaan aahe! — it is very good!) and settle up with बिल द्या (bill dya — please give the bill).

How Marathi Sentences Are Built: Word Order, Postpositions and Verb Endings

Translate English to Marathi · Learning Guides · Grammar

Marathi grammar sounds complicated from the outside, but its sentences are built from a few steady habits. Learn these habits and you can both decode translations from this site and start assembling your own sentences with confidence.

Habit one: the verb comes last

English order is subject–verb–object: "She reads a book." Marathi order is subject–object–verb: ती पुस्तक वाचते (ti pustak vaachte — literally "she book reads"). When you check a translation, read it backwards from the verb and the sentence suddenly makes sense. Time words like आज (aaj — today) usually sit early in the sentence, and the thing being emphasised often sits just before the verb.

Habit two: little words come after, not before

English puts "in", "on" and "for" before the noun. Marathi glues its helpers after the noun — these are postpositions. घर (ghar — house) becomes घरात (gharaat — in the house), घरावर (gharaavar — on the house), घराकडे (gharaakade — towards the house). "For me" is माझ्यासाठी (maajhyaasaathi). And "of" — possession — is shown by चा / ची / चे (chaa / chi / che), which changes with the gender of the thing possessed: रामचा भाऊ (raamchaa bhaau — Ram's brother), रामची बहीण (raamchi baheen — Ram's sister).

Habit three: three genders, everywhere

Every Marathi noun is masculine, feminine or neuter, and adjectives and verbs agree. मोठा कुत्रा (motha kutra — big dog, masculine), मोठी मांजर (mothi maanjar — big cat, feminine), मोठे घर (mothe ghar — big house, neuter). The same agreement reaches the verb: तो जातो (to jaato — he goes), ती जाते (ti jaate — she goes), ते जातात (te jaataat — they go). This is the single biggest difference from English, and the reason the accuracy guide tells you to check endings first when a translation looks odd.

Habit four: the verb ending tells you who

Marathi verbs change their tails to show tense, person and gender together. Take "speak", बोलणे (bolne): मी बोलतो (mi bolto — I speak, male), मी बोलते (mi bolte — I speak, female), तुम्ही बोलता (tumhi boltaa — you speak), ते बोलतात (te boltaat — they speak). Because the ending already says who, Marathi often drops the pronoun entirely in conversation — बोलतो alone is a complete sentence.

Habit five: questions and negatives are simple

As the questions guide shows, a statement becomes a yes–no question by adding का (kaa) at the end: तुम्ही येता का? (tumhi yetaa kaa? — are you coming?). Negatives use नाही (naahi — not): मला माहीत नाही (mala maahit naahi — I don't know), and "don't" or "not wanted" uses नको (nako): मला चहा नको (mala chahaa nako — I don't want tea). There is no separate word for "is" in many present-tense sentences: हे माझे घर (he maajhe ghar) simply means "this (is) my house".

Build your first sentences

Try the pattern yourself: pick a pronoun from the pronouns list, an object from the vocabulary guide, and a verb — then check your sentence in the translator. Example: मी मराठी शिकतो आहे (mi marathi shikto aahe — I am learning Marathi). If the translator agrees with you, you are no longer memorising Marathi — you are building it.
